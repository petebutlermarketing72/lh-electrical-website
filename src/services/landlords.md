Since 2020, private landlords in England have been legally required to hold a current EICR — every five years, or at change of tenancy. Penalties for getting it wrong are real, but the more common frustration we hear from landlords is patchy quality of reports from other contractors. We provide an EICR that's actually useful: a clear classification, a written report you can hand straight to your letting agent, and a fixed quote for any remedial work needed.

## What we cover

<ul class="checklist">
<li>Landlord EICRs for single properties and portfolios</li>
<li>Remedial work for C1/C2 coded issues, inside the 28-day window</li>
<li>Consumer unit and fuse board upgrades</li>
<li>Rewires between tenancies</li>
<li>Coordinated inspections across multiple properties</li>
<li>Fast turnaround reports for letting agents</li>
</ul>

## Keeping void periods short

For portfolio landlords and letting agents, timing matters. We'll coordinate inspections across multiple properties to keep access visits efficient, and prioritise remedial work so void periods between tenancies stay as short as possible.

## Common questions

<dl class="faq">
<dt>My EICR has come back unsatisfactory — what now?</dt>
<dd>You've got 28 days to address any C1 or C2 codes. If we carried out the inspection, we'll quote the remedial work directly and re-issue a clean certificate once it's done. If another contractor did the original report, we can still help — we'll review the findings and price the work.</dd>
<dt>How often do I need an EICR?</dt>
<dd>Every five years as standard, or sooner at a change of tenancy if the previous report is close to expiry.</dd>
<dt>Can you manage inspections across a whole portfolio?</dt>
<dd>Yes — we regularly coordinate multi-property programmes for landlords and letting agents to keep scheduling and reporting consistent.</dd>
