If you haven't had your electrics checked in a while, or you're buying a property and want to know what you're taking on, an inspection gives you a clear picture rather than a guess. We test to BS 7671 and give you a straightforward report — what's fine, what needs attention, and what's urgent.

## What we cover

<ul class="checklist">
<li>Electrical Installation Condition Reports (EICRs)</li>
<li>Pre-purchase and pre-lease property inspections</li>
<li>PAT testing (Portable Appliance Testing)</li>
<li>Commercial and industrial electrical testing</li>
<li>Post-incident inspections, following fire, flood or a fault</li>
<li>Fire alarm and emergency lighting testing</li>
</ul>

## What you'll get back

Every inspection comes with a clear, coded report (C1, C2, C3 or FI), explained in plain English rather than just left as a certificate. If anything needs fixing, we'll include a fixed quote for the remedial work — no pressure either way.

Renting the property out? See our <a href="/electrical-services/landlords/">landlord EICR page</a> for the specific legal requirements that apply to rental properties.

    <h2>Common questions</h2>
    <dl class="faq">
<dt>How long does an EICR take?</dt>
<dd>Typically 2 to 4 hours for a home, depending on size and the number of circuits. Commercial properties vary more — we'll give you a time estimate with the quote.</dd>
<dt>What do the codes on my report mean?</dt>
<dd>C1 means danger present, needing immediate attention. C2 is potentially dangerous and needs addressing soon. C3 is an improvement recommendation, not a failure.</dd>
<dt>Do you carry out PAT testing alongside an EICR?</dt>
<dd>Yes, we can combine both in the same visit for homes or businesses — just let us know when booking.</dd>
