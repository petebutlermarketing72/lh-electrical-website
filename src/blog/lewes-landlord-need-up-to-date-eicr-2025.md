<h2>What Is an EICR?</h2>
<p>An <strong>Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR)</strong> is a formal document issued after a qualified electrician inspects and tests the fixed electrical systems in a property. It identifies any safety issues, wear and tear, or non-compliance with current wiring regulations (BS 7671).</p>

<h2>Why Is EICR Renewal So Important?</h2>

<p><strong>✅ Legal Compliance</strong></p>
<p>Since July 2020, <strong>UK regulations require landlords to have a valid EICR every five years</strong>. Failing to comply can result in fines of up to <strong>£30,000</strong>.</p>

<p><strong>✅ Tenant Safety</strong></p>
<p>Outdated wiring or unsafe systems can lead to fires, electric shocks, and serious harm. Regular inspections help identify and fix hazards before they cause damage.</p>

<p><strong>✅ Insurance Validity</strong></p>
<p>Most landlord insurance policies require proof of an in-date EICR. An expired certificate could <strong>invalidate your cover</strong> in the event of a claim.</p>

<p><strong>✅ Protect Property &amp; Reputation</strong></p>
<p>A safe, well-maintained home is more attractive to tenants. Up-to-date certifications build trust, reduce complaints, and help retain good renters.</p>

<h2>What Happens If Repairs Are Needed?</h2>
<p>If an EICR flags urgent issues (coded C1 or C2), <strong>repairs must be completed within 28 days</strong>—or sooner if specified. Once fixed, you'll need written confirmation that the work has been carried out by a qualified electrician.</p>
