<h2>What Is a Consumer Unit?</h2>
<p>A consumer unit (also called a fuse box) distributes electricity throughout your home and protects circuits from overloads. Modern units feature:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>RCD protection</strong> - Cuts power in milliseconds to prevent electric shocks</li>
<li><strong>Circuit breakers (MCBs)</strong> - Replace old-style fuses</li>
<li><strong>Metal casing</strong> - Fire safety requirement since 2018</li>
</ul>
<p>Old fuse boxes with rewirable fuses lack these safety features and don't meet current regulations.</p>

<h2>Why Replace Your Consumer Unit?</h2>

<h3>It's a Legal Requirement</h3>
<p>The 18th Edition regulations (BS 7671:2018) require:</p>
<ul>
<li>RCD protection on all circuits</li>
<li>Metal enclosures (no plastic)</li>
<li>Surge protection in certain installations</li>
</ul>

<h3>Insurance Demands It</h3>
<p>Many insurers now require:</p>
<ul>
<li>Valid electrical certificates</li>
<li>Modern consumer units</li>
<li>Regular electrical testing (EICR)</li>
</ul>
<p>An outdated fuse box can invalidate your home insurance.</p>

<h3>Safety First</h3>
<p>Old consumer units increase risk of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Electric shocks</li>
<li>Electrical fires</li>
<li>System overloads</li>
</ul>
<p>Modern RCDs can prevent fatal accidents by cutting power in 40 milliseconds.</p>

<h2>Signs You Need a Replacement</h2>
<p>Replace your consumer unit if you notice:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Over 25 years old</strong> - Components deteriorate over time</li>
<li><strong>Rewirable fuses</strong> - Dangerous and outdated</li>
<li><strong>No RCD protection</strong> - Doesn't meet current standards</li>
<li><strong>Scorch marks or burning smell</strong> - Fire risk</li>
<li><strong>Wooden backing board</strong> - Common in older Lewes properties</li>
<li><strong>Frequent tripping</strong> - Overloaded or failing</li>
<li><strong>Adding EV charger</strong> - Old units can't handle the load</li>
<li><strong>Failed EICR</strong> - C1 or C2 codes mean replacement needed</li>
</ol>
